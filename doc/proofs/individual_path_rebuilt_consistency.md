# Consistency of both booleans on the path_rebuilt points

Claim. For each of `path_rebuilt_by_identity`, `path_rebuilt_by_plus_minus_small` and
`path_rebuilt_by_plus_minus_tiny`, with M the point's program, both scored tests are
consistent with lambda beta eta:

- boolean(true): T = \a. M a y, and
- boolean(false): T = \a. M y a (y free).

It suffices (as in the false_only proof, `individual_false_only_consistency.md`) that lambda beta eta + (M = v) is
consistent for v = true and for v = false: that theory proves T = \a. v a y = \a. a,
resp. \a. v y a = \a. a.

## 1. The general lemma (the one of the false_only proof, with v general)

Let v be true or false, w the other one. Let G be a set of closed terms with

- (G1) G is closed under one-step beta eta reduction;
- (G2) every term of G other than v is an application;
- (G3) if N is in G and an occurrence of a subterm N' of N is in G, N with that
  occurrence replaced by v is in G;
- (G4) w is not in G (it follows from (G2)).

Then lambda beta eta + {N = v : N in G} is consistent. The proof is word for word the
false_only one with false replaced by v: rho: N -> v for N in G, N /= v; parallel rho has
the diamond property by (G3); a beta eta step and a parallel rho step close with at most
one parallel rho step on one side and at most one beta eta step on the other, using (G1)
for a redex inside a collapsed occurrence and (G2) for "a collapsed occurrence is never
the operator of a redex", and the eta case `\z. N z` giving `\z. v z ->eta v`; by
Hindley-Rosen with the confluence of beta eta, R = beta eta U rho is confluent; v and w
are R-normal (beta eta normal, their closed subterms are themselves, w is not in G), so
they are not R-convertible.

So it remains to give, for each point and each v, a set G that contains M and satisfies
(G1) to (G3).

## 2. The program

From `contest_hard_clauses.pl` and `prelude_clauses.pl` (`^` is lambda; T = \t. \f. t,
F = \t. \f. f, Nil = \n. \c. n, Cons = \h. \t. \n. \c. c h t, Or = \a. \b. a T b,
I = \x. x, Y = \f. (\p. f (p p)) (\q. f (q q)), 0 = \z. \s. z):

    Safe = Y S,   S = \self. \node. node (\bad. \succ. bad F (Y A succ)),
                  A = \any. \list. list F (\h. \t. Or (self h) (any t))
    Gen  = Y G,   G = \self. \arg. \n. n F (Cons (self (R arg)) Nil)
    M    = Safe (Gen 0)

with R = I for `path_rebuilt_by_identity` and R = \x. Diff (Plus x k) k (k the numeral 3
resp. 1) for the two plus_minus points. Note A has `self` free (bound by S), and in S
the fixpoint `Y A` is built afresh under each `self`.

Two families of source code occur: SAFE CODE (S, A, Or, and the constants T, F, Nil
written in them) and PATH CODE (G, R, Cons, Nil, 0, and the numeral code of Plus and
Diff). A closed term built by reduction from path code alone contains no safe code, and
conversely: reduction only copies and erases subterms of the redex.

## 3. Components

Each component set below is the set of all beta eta reducts of the terms listed, hence
closed under beta eta reduction by definition; each is built from one code family only.

- P, the safe instances: reducts of Safe.
- N, the nodes: reducts of Gen' a, Gen' a reduct of Gen, a in Args, where Args is the
  least set containing 0, closed under beta eta reduction and under a |-> R a.
- L, the lists: reducts of Cons h Nil, h in N.
- C: reducts of `\bad. \succ. bad F (Y A_Q succ)`, Q in P (A_Q is A with self := Q).
- C1: reducts of `\succ. F F (Y A_Q succ)`, Q in P.
- Z, the any instances: reducts of Y A_Q, Q in P.
- H: reducts of `\h. \t. Or (Q h) (Z t)`, Q in P, Z in Z.
- W: reducts of Z t, Z in Z (open in the variable t).
- K: reducts of Z Nil, Z in Z.
- O: the reducts of Or, which are Or and \a. a T.

The ABSTRACTION FORMS of the components, by inspection (a variable in head position
blocks the root, and Y-built cores reduce only by unfolding):

- an abstraction in P is \node. node C, C in C;
- an abstraction in N is \n. n F L, L in L (every node is convertible to Gen' a and
  unfolds to this form);
- an abstraction in L is \n. \c. c h Nil, h in N;
- an abstraction in C is \bad. C1', and C1'[bad := F] is in C1;
- an abstraction in C1 is \succ. E with E, after succ := L, one of
  F F N1, I N1 or N1, where N1 is `Z L` or `L F H` (Z in Z, H in H): the any-call on
  the successor list, possibly one step on;
- an abstraction in Z is \list. list F H, H in H;
- an abstraction in H is \h. H1 where H1, after h := h0 (h0 in N), is one of the forms
  of H-one below with its slot holding Q h0 or a reduct of it;
- the only abstraction in K is F.

## 4. The set G

The set G(v) is generated by the productions below, where m ranges over G(v) (the SLOT),
and the other letters over the components as named. Productions marked (Z) contain both
code families; those marked (C) are COLLAPSE STATES, which reduce to v.

    m ::= Q N0                     Q in P, N0 in N            (Z) the safe call
        | N0 C0                    N0 in N, C0 in C           (Z)
        | C0 F L0                  C0 in C, L0 in L           (Z)
        | C10 L0                   C10 in C1, L0 in L         (Z)
        | F F m                                               (Z or C, as m)
        | I m                                                 (Z or C, as m)
        | Z0 L0                    Z0 in Z, L0 in L           (Z)
        | L0 F H0                  H0 in H                    (Z)
        | (\c. c h0 Nil) H0        h0 in N                    (Z)
        | H0 h0 Nil                                           (Z)
        | H1 Nil                   H1 in H-one                (Z or C, as its slot)
        | O0 m K0                  O0 in O, K0 in K           (Z or C, as m)
        | (\b. m T b) K0                                      (Z or C, as m)
        | m T K0                                              (Z or C, as m)
        | v                                                   (C)
        | (\f. T) K0               only for v = true          (C)
        | I K0, K0                 only for v = false         (C)

    H-one ::= \t. O0 m W0 | \t. (\b. m T b) W0 | \t. m T W0            W0 in W
            | \t. (\f. T) W0 | \t. T                   (v = true)
            | \t. I W0 | \t. W0 | Z0                   (v = false)

(`m T K0` is `(m T) K0`. The alternative of a production is determined by its slot:
a (Z) production is one whose slot holds a (Z) term, all the way down.)

M = Safe (Gen 0) is the production `Q N0` (Q = Safe, N0 = Gen 0), so M is in G(v).

## 5. (G1): closed under one-step beta eta

A redex of a term of G(v) lies inside a component (the result stays in the component),
inside the slot (induction), or at a production: by the abstraction forms of Section 3,
a production has a redex at its root only when its operator part is an abstraction, and
then:

    Q N0            -> N0 C0                    (Q = \node. node C0; node not free in C0)
    N0 C0           -> C0 F L0                  (N0 = \n. n F L0)
    C0 F L0         -> C10 L0                   (C0 = \bad. C1'; C10 = C1'[bad := F])
    C10 L0          -> F F m | I m | m          (m = Z L0 or L0 F H0, one step on)
    F F m           -> I m                      (the redex F F)
    I m             -> m
    Z0 L0           -> L0 F H0                  (Z0 = \list. list F H0)
    L0 F H0         -> (\c. c h0 Nil) H0        (L0 = \n. \c. c h0 Nil)
    (\c. c h0 Nil) H0 -> H0 h0 Nil
    H0 h0 Nil       -> H1 Nil                   (H0 = \h. H1'; H1 = H1'[h := h0])
    H1 Nil          -> the body with t := Nil: O0 m K0, (\b. m T b) K0, m T K0,
                       (\f. T) K0, T, I K0 or K0, W0[t := Nil] being in K
    O0 m K0         -> (\b. m T b) K0           (O0 = Or)
                     | m T K0                   (O0 = \a. a T)
    (\b. m T b) K0  -> m T K0                   (beta, and eta on \b. (m T) b)
    m T K0          -> (\f. T) K0 | I K0        (m = v, the only abstraction in G(v);
                                                 T T -> \f. T, F T -> I)
    (\f. T) K0      -> T                        (= v, v = true)
    I K0            -> K0                       (v = false)

Inside the H-one bodies the same steps happen under \t (and eta on \b. (m T) b). The
eta redexes at production level are `\b. m T b` (b not free in m T), listed, and, for
v = false, `\t. W0` when W0 is `Z0 t` itself, which eta-reduces to Z0 (an H-one form;
`Z0 Nil` is then in K). No other body `\t. ...` is an eta redex: its last argument is
a reduct of Z t, which is never t (an any instance applied to t reduces to `t F H0`,
not to t).

## 6. (G2): every term of G(v) but v is an application

Every production other than v is an application (for v = false, K0 ranges over K, whose
only abstraction is F = v).

## 7. (G3): closed under collapsing a member

Let N' be an occurrence in N (both in G(v)) of a term of G(v).

- If N' is a (C) term, it reduces to v: v itself; `(\f. T) K0 -> T`; `I K0 -> K0 ->> F`;
  and each slot production with a (C) slot reduces to its slot's collapse (`F F m -> I m
  -> m`, `O0 m K0 ->> m T K0`, `m T K0` with m ->> v reaching `v T K0 ->> v` for both
  values of v, `H1 Nil ->> v` likewise). Replacing N' by v is then a beta reduction in
  place, so the result is in G(v) by (G1).
- If N' is a (Z) term, it has no weak head normal form (below). Every closed subterm of
  a component element has one: the components compute with Y-built cores (which reach
  an abstraction by one unfolding), nodes (convertible to Gen' a, which unfolds to
  `\n. n F L0`), lists, the numerals of Args (all convertible to 0, since R a ->> a),
  the any-calls on Nil (K, whose weak head normal form is F) and the constants; the
  any-calls on a non-empty list occur in components only with the list a variable. So
  N' is not inside a component. Nor is it a proper part of a production other than the
  slot: the parts `F F`, `I`, `O0`, `\b. m T b`, `m T`, `C0 F`, `L0 F`, `H0 h0` and the
  like match no production (their operator is a component or a constant, and no
  production has that shape), and the slot is the only place a term of G(v) is
  admitted. So N' is N itself or sits in a slot; a slot admits any term of G(v), v
  included, so the replacement is in G(v).

  No (Z) term has a weak head normal form. Every (Z) term is convertible to M: the
  productions follow the reduction of `Q N0` with N0 convertible to Gen 0 (every a in
  Args is convertible to 0), and a slot holding a (Z) term m in `m T K0` gives a term
  convertible to `M T F`, which M itself reduces to (M ->> (Q h0) T K0 with Q h0
  convertible to M and K0 to F). By the table of Section 5, the weak head reduction of
  every (Z) term reaches, after at least one step, a term `m T K0` (or `F F m`, `I m`,
  ...) whose weak head reduction begins with that of its slot m, again a (Z) term. If
  some (Z) term had a weak head normal form, one with the shortest weak head reduction
  would have a (Z) slot with a strictly shorter one, a contradiction.

## 8. Conclusion

For v = true and v = false, G(v) satisfies (G1) to (G3) and contains M; by the lemma,
lambda beta eta + (M = v) is consistent, and so are both scored tests of each of the
three points. The argument does not depend on R beyond R being path code; it uses no
atom and no judge reading.
